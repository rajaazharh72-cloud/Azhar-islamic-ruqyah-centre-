What we treat

Afflictions addressed by Ruqyah

Each of these is affirmed by the Qur'an or the authentic Sunnah. None of them harms except by Allah's permission — and none of them is a mark against the one afflicted.

Black Magic

السِّحْر

Sihr is real, and the Qur'an affirms it plainly — but it harms none except by Allah's permission. It is a sin committed against you, not a stain upon you. Treatment is by the Book of Allah, patience, and Allah's decree.

Recognised signs
  • Signs may include a sudden, unexplained aversion to someone previously loved, or to the home itself
  • Persistent physical complaints that repeated medical investigation cannot account for
  • A heaviness or blockage that settles specifically upon prayer, wudu or Qur'an recitation
  • Long-running obstruction in provision, work or marriage without any worldly explanation
  • Disturbed appetite, unexplained weight change, or a taste in the mouth that comes and goes
  • Agitation, tightness in the chest or a sense of dread that lifts and returns in cycles
  • A marked reaction — distress, drowsiness, nausea or agitation — during the recitation of Qur'an

Evil Eye

الْعَيْن وَالْحَسَد

The Prophet ﷺ affirmed that the eye is real. It may come from an enemy through envy, or from a loved one through unguarded admiration. It is among the most common afflictions and often among the most quickly relieved, in sha' Allah.

Recognised signs
  • Symptoms may include a sudden decline in health, energy or fortune following praise or public attention
  • Excessive yawning, watering eyes or heavy drowsiness during Qur'an or du'a
  • Headaches that move about the head and resist ordinary treatment
  • Loss of appetite, pallor, or a persistent tiredness that sleep does not repair
  • Sudden dislike of one's own appearance, work or possessions without cause
  • Recurrent minor accidents, breakages or setbacks clustered in a short period
  • In children: unexplained crying, night waking, fever without illness, or a change in temperament

Jinn Possession of the Body

مَسُّ الْجِنّ

That a jinn may afflict a human body is affirmed by the Qur'an and the Sunnah, though it is far rarer than fear and the internet suggest. Most of what is attributed to it is medical or psychological, and must be investigated as such first.

Recognised signs
  • Signs may include episodes of altered awareness with partial or no memory afterwards
  • Strong, involuntary aversion or agitation specifically at the adhan or Qur'an
  • Speech, movements or a voice during episodes that the person does not recognise as their own
  • Repeated frightening dreams involving animals, being chased, falling, or the deceased
  • Unusual physical strength, rigidity or convulsive movement during recitation
  • Whispering, intrusive thoughts urging disbelief, despair or self-harm
  • Pressure or constriction in the chest and throat, particularly at night
  • Note: seizures, dissociation and several psychiatric conditions present similarly. A doctor must be seen.

Possession & Disturbances of the Home

سُكْنَى الْجِنِّ فِي الْبَيْت

A house left empty of Qur'an and adhkar may become a place where harm settles. The Prophet ﷺ said that Shaytan flees a house in which Surat al-Baqarah is recited — the remedy begins with the household, not with the healer.

Recognised signs
  • May include a persistent unease or oppression felt in one particular room, corner or stairway
  • Sounds without source: footsteps, knocking, movement of furniture, doors opening or closing
  • Odours appearing and vanishing with no origin — smoke, decay, or something sweet and heavy
  • Animals or small children reacting to a space adults find unremarkable
  • Sleep disturbed only in that home and restored when the family stays elsewhere
  • Recurring quarrels that begin without cause and cool the moment the family leaves the house
  • Electrical items, lights or taps behaving erratically after competent inspection finds no fault

Intelligent Hauntings

الْجِنُّ الْعَابِث

Some disturbances appear to respond — to speech, to entry, to a person in particular. This is not the soul of the deceased returning; the dead do not return. It is the meddling of a jinn, and it is addressed with Qur'an, not conversation.

Recognised signs
  • May include disturbances that appear to react to speech, movement or the presence of one household member
  • Objects moved, hidden or returned in a manner that seems deliberate rather than random
  • Being touched, held down or called by name, most often between sleep and waking
  • Escalation when the family is frightened, and quiet when the house is filled with Qur'an
  • Apparitions taking the form of a person, child or animal, sometimes claiming to be a deceased relative
  • One member of the household targeted consistently while others are untouched
  • We do not speak with it, question it, or seek information from it. Any such dialogue is forbidden.

Domestic & Marital Affairs

التَّفْرِيقُ بَيْنَ الزَّوْجَيْن

The Qur'an names separation between husband and wife as the very harm sorcerers seek. Yet not every troubled marriage is bewitched — and to say otherwise is to excuse people from repentance and effort. We look honestly at both.

Recognised signs
  • May include love turning to sudden, causeless revulsion at the sight, voice or scent of the spouse
  • Trivial disputes that escalate beyond all proportion and are regretted immediately afterwards
  • A blanket of coldness that lifts when the spouses are apart and returns the moment they are together
  • Each spouse seeing the other in a distorted, magnified or repellent way
  • Marital intimacy obstructed with no medical cause after proper examination
  • Proposals of marriage repeatedly collapsing at the final stage without reason
  • Children in the household becoming unsettled, fearful or sleepless alongside the discord
  • Note: neglect, harshness, addiction and abuse are not sihr. They require accountability, counsel, and sometimes protection.

The method

Ruqyah Shar'iyyah, step by step

Nothing here is hidden from you. This is the whole of it, in the order it happens.

01

Listening first

Before a single verse is recited, we sit with you and hear the whole account — when it began, what has been tried, what the doctors have said, what the household has witnessed. Nothing is dismissed as too strange or too small. Where the picture points toward a medical or psychological cause, we will say so plainly and direct you to seek that care. Honesty at this stage protects you from years of treating the wrong thing.

02

Returning to the foundations

Ruqyah is not a substitute for what Allah has already obliged. We begin by restoring the ground beneath you: the five prayers guarded in their times, tawbah for what has passed, the morning and evening adhkar, a daily portion of Qur'an, and lawful earnings and food. Many afflictions loosen at this stage alone, before any session is held, by Allah's permission.

03

The recitation

We recite the speech of Allah over you — al-Fatihah, Ayat al-Kursi, the closing verses of al-Baqarah, the verses concerning sihr, al-Ikhlas and the two surahs of refuge, together with the supplications authentically reported from the Prophet ﷺ. It is recited in clear Arabic, aloud, with meaning understood and intention sincere. There is no secret portion. You may record it, learn it, and recite it upon yourself thereafter.

04

The prophetic means

Alongside recitation we use only what the Sunnah affirms: blowing with a light spray of breath, water and olive oil recited over, black seed, honey, and the recorded prophetic remedies for the eye and for sihr. Nothing is worn, buried, burned or hung. Nothing is written to be carried. If a treatment cannot be traced to the Book or the Sunnah, it has no place in this center.

05

Fortifying the home

Where the affliction touches the household, the household is treated. Surat al-Baqarah recited in the home, the adhkar of entering and leaving, of eating and of sleep, images and instruments of disobedience removed, and the family taught to recite for themselves. A house maintained by Qur'an does not stay easy to trouble.

06

Patience, follow-up, and handing it back to Allah

Some are relieved in a single sitting; others are given a longer road, and that road is itself a purification and a raising of rank. We follow up with you, adjust, and keep reciting. But we do not count down to a promised day, because no one on this earth knows it. The cure was never in our hands. We hold to the means and return the outcome to Ash-Shafi, and that is where peace is actually found.

Permissible, not sorcery

What is permitted, and what we will never do

The scholars are agreed that ruqyah is permissible — indeed recommended — when three conditions are met. Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani gathered them in Fath al-Bari, his commentary on Sahih al-Bukhari, reporting the consensus of the scholars: that the ruqyah be by the speech of Allah, His names and His attributes; that it be in Arabic or in words whose meaning is known and understood; and that one believe it has no effect of itself, but only by the decree of Allah. Everything we do stands inside those three lines. Everything outside them we refuse, however much it is asked of us, and however desperate the request.

What Ruqyah Shar'iyyah is

  • It is the speech of Allah — Qur'an recited aloud upon the afflicted, together with His beautiful names and His attributes, and the supplications authentically reported from the Prophet ﷺ.
  • It is in clear Arabic, its meaning known to the one reciting and explained to the one receiving. Nothing unintelligible is uttered, because unintelligible speech is precisely where shirk hides.
  • It is held to be a means and never a cause. The verse does not heal of itself; Allah heals by it. Believing otherwise is the very door the Prophet ﷺ closed.
  • It is done openly. You may hear every word, record the session, bring a mahram or family member, and ask why any particular verse was chosen.
  • It is free of anything the Shari'ah forbids: no isolation with a non-mahram, no touching what may not be touched, no removal of clothing, no darkness or incense or ritual staging.
  • It is joined to obedience, not offered as an alternative to it — prayer, tawbah, adhkar, lawful earnings, and a household kept upon remembrance.

What we will never do

  • We never summon, command, question, contract with, or seek the aid of jinn. This is sorcery itself, and seeking help from jinn is forbidden without exception — no matter the intention, and no matter how effective it is claimed to be.
  • We never write, sell, tie or hang amulets, ta'weez or tama'im, nor anything containing letters, numbers, squares, symbols, or disjointed words. The Prophet ﷺ was severe regarding such things, and their harm to a person's tawhid outweighs any relief they seem to bring.
  • We never claim knowledge of the unseen — not the name of the one who harmed you, not their whereabouts, not what will come. Whoever claims this is a fortune-teller, and the Prophet ﷺ warned of what is lost by believing him.
  • We never counter sorcery with sorcery. Undoing a knot by means of another sorcerer, however it is dressed up, is nushrah of the forbidden kind, described by the Prophet ﷺ as being from the work of Shaytan.
  • We never guarantee a cure, sell a number of sessions in advance as a package, or price certainty. Certainty is not ours to sell.
  • We never tell you to stop a prescribed medical treatment, abandon a doctor, or delay urgent care. Anyone who tells you otherwise is endangering your body in the name of your religion.

The full verses recited in every session — al-Fatihah, Ayat al-Kursi, the closing verses of al-Baqarah, the three Quls and more — are gathered, with translation and authenticated sources, in the Dua Book.

Questions

What people ask before they come

Is Ruqyah permissible in Islam?

Yes — and it is from the Sunnah. The Prophet ﷺ recited upon himself and upon his Companions, was recited upon, and approved of ruqyah performed by others. The scholars are agreed on its permissibility under three conditions, gathered by Ibn Hajar in Fath al-Bari: that it be by the speech of Allah, His names and attributes; that it be in Arabic or in words of understood meaning; and that one believe it has no effect of itself, but only by Allah's decree. Ruqyah that meets these conditions is worship. Ruqyah that abandons them is something else entirely, whatever it is called.

Can Ruqyah be performed remotely?

Yes. Ruqyah is recitation and du'a, and neither requires the reciter to be in the room — the Prophet ﷺ supplicated for people who were absent from him. Remote sessions by call or video are valid, in sha' Allah, and are often the wiser choice for sisters, for those far away, and for those too unwell to travel. In-person sessions allow closer assessment and are preferred where the home itself is involved. Neither is more powerful than the other; the power was never in proximity.

What should I prepare before a session?

Come in a state of wudu and in modest, loose clothing. Perform your obligatory prayers on time in the days beforehand, make tawbah sincerely, and begin the morning and evening adhkar even if imperfectly. Bring a mahram or family member if you wish — you are encouraged to. Bring any medical reports or diagnoses you have. Eat lightly. Most importantly, come with your hope placed in Allah rather than in the session: it is He who cures, and He is nearer to you than the one reciting.

How many sessions will I need?

We do not know, and we will not pretend to. Some people are relieved in a single sitting; others walk a longer road, and that road carries its own reward and expiation. Anyone who tells you the number before meeting you is selling something. What we can tell you is that we will not stretch treatment out unnecessarily, we will tell you honestly if we believe ruqyah is not what your situation requires, and our aim throughout is to make you capable of reciting upon yourself so that you need us less, not more.

What are your fees?

Every figure is published openly under Fees — consultation, follow-up, home visits, and how to pay — so that you know before you come and never have to ask awkwardly. The fee covers the consultation, the guidance, the time and the Ruqyah session itself, and nothing more. Scholars have permitted taking a fee for ruqyah, on the evidence of the Companion who recited al-Fatihah upon a tribal chief and whose payment the Prophet ﷺ approved. But we will never charge for a guaranteed result, sell packages of sessions in advance, or price by the severity of your fear. If cost is a barrier, tell us in confidence — we would rather find a way with you than have you stay away.

Should I also see a doctor?

Yes, always, and we will keep saying it. The Prophet ﷺ commanded us to seek treatment, and told us that Allah sent down no illness without also sending down its cure. A great many symptoms attributed to sihr, 'ayn or jinn are in truth anaemia, thyroid disorder, epilepsy, migraine, sleep apnoea, depression, anxiety, psychosis, or the effects of medication. These are real illnesses with real treatments and no shame attached. Ruqyah and medicine are not rivals — both are means, and both work only by Allah's permission. Never stop a prescribed treatment for us or for anyone.

Do you give amulets or ta'weez?

No. Not written, not tied, not worn, not hung, not buried, not carried in a wallet or pinned to a child. This includes amulets said to contain Qur'an. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever hangs an amulet, may Allah not fulfil his need for him — and among the Companions and the scholars who came after, the majority forbade them outright, precisely because they open a door to attaching the heart to an object instead of to Allah. What we give you instead is the recitation itself, and the adhkar, so the protection lives on your tongue and in your heart, where it cannot be lost or taken.
